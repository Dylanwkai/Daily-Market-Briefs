## 2. Theme Tracker

### 2.1 fed-path — US Fed path & inflation — STABLE (odds barely moved, but tonight's setup is historically unusual either way)
- **What changed:** CME FedWatch's hike odds sit at roughly 38%, essentially unchanged from Tuesday's 35-38% range. The new wrinkle: Kalshi's prediction market prices only about 24-25% odds of a hike — a meaningfully wider CME/Kalshi gap than usual. Bloomberg flagged that the Fed has never delivered a hike with swaps-implied odds this low since it began tracking the measure in 2008, meaning a hike tonight would be genuinely unprecedented by that yardstick. Logan (Dallas) and Hammack (Cleveland) remain the reporting-flagged likely hawkish dissenters if the committee holds. The decision (2pm ET) and Warsh's press conference (2:30pm ET) land at roughly 2am/2:30am SGT Thursday — still hours away as this goes to print.
- **Market effect:** The US 10-year yield has stopped falling — roughly flat at 4.61-4.63% after three straight sessions lower — and the dollar index (DXY) is similarly flat near 101.3, both effectively parked into tonight's decision.
- **Concept:** CME FedWatch derives odds from Fed-funds futures — professional money betting with real capital on the rate path. Kalshi is a retail-accessible prediction market where anyone can take a position. When the two diverge this much, one side is likely capturing information the other isn't: futures markets may be pricing in some probability that the hawkish dissenters actually carry the vote, while retail money on Kalshi is reading Warsh's hawkish rhetoric as more bark than bite. Tonight's actual vote count settles which read was right.

### 2.2 hormuz-oil — Hormuz / Iran war — TURNING (from de-escalating to actively escalating, the sharpest reversal yet)
- **What changed:** Overnight, Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired multiple ballistic missiles at a base reportedly housing US forces in Jordan; CENTCOM said every missile was intercepted. The US and Saudi militaries retaliated Tuesday, striking Iran-backed militia logistics and weapons sites in eastern Iraq. Trump warned of "a force that has never been seen" if Iran strikes again. This directly reverses Monday-Tuesday's diplomatic track — Iran's FM Araghchi's direct calls with his Saudi and Omani counterparts, and Oman's proposed "regional framework" (modeled on the Malacca Strait's joint-oversight approach) for Hormuz security — which continues, but now runs in parallel with active combat rather than in place of it. Separately, the Houthi Bab al-Mandeb embargo deepened further: strait traffic has fallen 56% since the embargo began, the Houthis claimed fresh strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities at Jizan and Yanbu, and a Saudi-flagged tanker took damage — most major carriers are now rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope.
- **Market effect:** Brent surged roughly 7-8% to about $89.60-90.70 and WTI jumped 6.4-6.6% to about $84.30-84.50 — a near-complete round trip of Monday and Tuesday's combined ~12% de-escalation decline, in a single session.
- **Concept:** Oil prices carry an embedded war-risk premium that isn't really about current supply — Hormuz hasn't closed and no tanker has been sunk this week — it's a probability-weighted bet across a range of outcomes, from "this settles down again" to "this becomes a regional war that closes a chokepoint." When new information shifts the odds of the bad tail even a little (an actual missile exchange, rather than diplomatic talk), price can move a lot, because it's repricing the whole distribution of outcomes, not just what literally happened overnight. That's why a night with zero barrels physically disrupted can still move Brent nearly 8%.

## 6. Idea Lab — [[ASML]] (NASDAQ: ASML)

*A deliberate shift in register from Korea's memory-chip crisis to the other end of the semiconductor supply chain: the single company that makes the machines Samsung, SK Hynix and TSMC need just to manufacture a chip in the first place, and whose stock is now caught up in this week's China-self-sufficiency scare.*

**6.1 How it makes money:** ASML designs and sells lithography systems — the machines that print microscopic circuit patterns onto silicon wafers — to the world's leading-edge chipmakers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, Micron, SK Hynix). Its EUV (extreme ultraviolet) systems have no commercial competitor and cost upward of $200 million each; its older DUV (deep ultraviolet) line is more contested but still dominant. A high-margin recurring services and upgrade business, tied to ASML's large installed base, adds roughly a quarter of revenue on top of new-machine sales. Three KPIs matter most: (1) bookings and order backlog, the clearest forward demand signal in a business where machines are ordered years ahead of delivery; (2) gross margin (currently around 52%), which reflects how much pricing power ASML retains as a near-monopoly supplier; and (3) China's share of revenue, the geopolitical exposure line investors are watching most closely this week. ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/29/threat-china-creeps-up-asml-is-dip-stock-buy/))

> [!bullish]
> **6.2 Bull:** The scale gap between ASML and China's new domestic entrant is enormous — the state-backed Shanghai DUV venture is reportedly making around 5 machines in 2026, targeting 20 in 2027, against ASML's roughly 130 machines a year in DUV alone and an order book effectively sold out through 2027. Q2 revenue was €9.33bn with full-year guidance raised to €43-45bn, and China's share of ASML sales actually fell to 14% (from 19% in Q1) — reducing, not increasing, the company's China-self-sufficiency exposure just as the scare hit the stock.

> [!bearish]
> **6.3 Bear:** The stock has fallen roughly 12-13% over two sessions on the psychological blow of China proving, for the first time, that it can build immersion DUV tools domestically — even at tiny current volume, that breaks the "unassailable moat" narrative that has justified ASML's premium multiple for years, and a state-directed industry with Beijing's financial backing has a track record of scaling manufacturing capacity far faster than market-based forecasts assume. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/asml-slides-after-report-of-china-beginning-duv-tool-production))

**6.4 Differentiating question:** Is the market right to treat "China can now make some DUV tools" as a genuine multi-year threat to ASML's moat, or is this the same priced-for-perfection overreaction pattern that hit Samsung and SK Hynix this week — a real technical milestone that's still several orders of magnitude away in volume from mattering to ASML's order book? The way to find out: track whether the Shanghai venture's 2027 production target (20 machines) gets confirmed or revised, and watch ASML's next order-backlog disclosure for any sign that its own Chinese customers are actually shifting orders toward the domestic tools rather than merely using the news as a bargaining chip.
