## 2. Theme Tracker

### 2.1 fed-path — US Fed path & inflation — STABLE (dovish reset holding, broadening into the equity rally)
- **What changed:** Friday's full US session — which hadn't yet closed when Friday morning's briefing published — confirmed the jobs-shock dovish repricing broadened rather than faded. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq both closed at records, the rate-sensitive Russell 2000 rallied 1.10%, and roughly 85% of S&P 500 Q2 reporters have now beaten estimates. Yields ticked modestly up off Friday morning's post-NFP lows — the 2-year to ~4.19% from 4.16%, the 10-year to ~4.64% from 4.61% — a partial retracement, not a reversal.
- **Market effect:** S&P 500 +0.62% to 7,757.64 (record), Nasdaq +1.30% to 26,690.62 — best week since mid-April (S&P +~3.5%, Nasdaq +~5%). DXY 99.56, -0.37% on the day.
- **Concept:** A single data point can trigger a dovish repricing, but the first session's reaction only tells you the market's initial read; the following session tells you whether that read survives once more participants have positioned around it. Friday's broadening — small caps and earnings-sensitive names joining bonds and gold — is a stronger signal of genuine conviction than the initial spike, because a one-day move can be narrow fast-money positioning while a multi-day, cross-asset confirmation usually reflects real capital reallocating.

### 2.2 hormuz-oil — Hormuz / Iran war — TURNING (deal-proximity rhetoric collides with a fresh strike)
- **What changed:** Iran's FM Araghchi said Saturday negotiators are "very close" to an Oman maritime transit-corridor deal, but stressed it won't reopen the Strait immediately. IRGC spokesman Sardar Mohebi separately said full reopening isn't tied to the Oman talks at all — it hinges on Washington "fully accepting" Iran's terms, which the Supreme National Security Council specified as US compensation payments and an end to the naval blockade. Iran's parliament still hasn't brought the restrictive shipping-corridor bill to a final floor vote. Hours after Araghchi's comments, UKMTO flagged a fresh strike: an unidentified projectile hit a cargo vessel roughly 33km off Khasab, Oman, Saturday — fire extinguished, no casualties, the latest in a string of tanker incidents this month. Separately, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed a "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement" mutual-defense pact Friday, and Houthi strikes on Saudi/Yemen targets continued (two civilians killed near Marib) — both widen the regional risk premium independent of Hormuz itself.
- **Market effect:** Oil reversed Friday morning's dip to close the week higher: Brent +1.29% to ~$83.55, WTI +1.15% to ~$78.18, as the fresh tanker strike offset the demand-side jobs narrative that had pulled crude lower Friday morning.
- **Concept:** "Very close to a deal" and "a resolved risk" are not the same statement, and oil prices the gap between them. Iran's two-gate framing — an Oman corridor as a separate, lesser step from full Strait reopening, which itself requires unrelated US concessions — tells the market there's no single event that clears the risk premium in one move. That's why a strike and a conciliatory diplomatic statement can land the same day without contradiction: the market prices a probability-weighted range of outcomes, not the tone of the latest headline.

### 2.3 ai-capex — AI capex cycle — STRENGTHENING as dispersion, narrowing but unresolved
- **What changed:** Friday's full session showed the memory-chip pullback becoming more selective rather than ending — Micron fell only ~2% and SanDisk ~3% (both seen as insulated by HBM4 dominance and multiyear contracts), narrower moves than mid-week's rout. SK Hynix, though, fell further (~5%) after its board approved $38bn for two new DRAM-heavy fabs and deferred its shareholder-return decision to Q3 — the market read a large new capacity commitment as a 2027-28 oversupply risk, not a confidence signal, the same "good news is bad news" dynamic this theme has tracked all week. On the other side of the split, Nvidia and the broader chip complex (the SOXX semiconductor ETF +7% on the week) rallied hard after Elon Musk confirmed SpaceX will build exclusively on Nvidia's Vera Rubin architecture for orbital AI compute. A new variable emerged Friday: Bloomberg reported the US Commerce Department/BIS is reviewing Chinese firms' offshore and remote-rental access to Nvidia chips — not yet stock-moving, but a fresh regulatory overhang on the AI-hardware supply chain.
- **Market effect:** Kospi -0.60% to 6,258.77 (SK Hynix -~5%, Samsung +0.2%); Seagate -7% on profit-taking after a +210% YTD run; Nasdaq +1.30% to a record the same day, powered partly by Nvidia's +10% weekly run.
- **Concept:** A capacity-expansion announcement is genuinely ambiguous information. It can signal confidence that demand will keep growing (bullish) or the start of an oversupply cycle if every competitor expands at once and demand doesn't keep pace (bearish). Which reading wins depends on where the market thinks the cycle stands: early, when more supply is capacity the world still needs, or late, when more supply is what eventually crashes prices. SK Hynix's selloff on a $38bn capex commitment says the market currently leans toward "late."

### 2.4 bond-regime — Bond market regime — STABLE (dovish-repricing trade extending across assets)
- **What changed:** Gold extended Thursday-Friday's rally further, closing near $4,400 (+2.33% Friday, +4.8% on the week) — still near record territory — even as the VIX held at a multi-week low (14.90) and equities notched records across the US and Europe. Bitcoin firmed further to ~$65,100 (+2.8% on the week). Record-high risk assets, a still-climbing gold price, and subdued volatility all at once is the same dovish-Fed-repricing dynamic flagged in Friday morning's briefing — now confirmed by a full week of price action rather than a single session.
- **Market effect:** Gold +4.8% on the week to ~$4,400; Bitcoin +2.8% on the week to ~$65,100; VIX 14.90; STOXX 600, S&P 500 and Nasdaq all closed the week at records.
- **Concept:** A genuinely dovish repricing should show up consistently across assets that respond to falling real yields — gold (which pays no interest, so becomes relatively more attractive as rate-cut odds rise), risk assets (which get a lower discount rate on future earnings), and volatility (which tends to fall when the policy path gets less uncertain, not more). This week checked all three boxes at once, a more convincing signal than any single market's move — the real test is whether it survives Wednesday's US CPI print, the next data point that could confirm or unwind the whole trade.

## 5. Deep Dive — Rockstar's Founder Builds a Stake in Celsius and Demands the CEO's Ouster

**5.1 What happened:** [[Celsius Holdings]] reported Q2 2026 revenue of $817.9m (+11% y/y) and adjusted EPS of $0.36, both below consensus ($886.0m and $0.43), with the flagship Celsius brand's own sales down roughly 12% y/y and gross margin compressing from 51.5% to 48.1%. Shares fell 18% Thursday. [[Alani Nu]], the energy-drink brand Celsius acquired and folded into [[PepsiCo]]'s distribution system, delivered $364.4m in revenue (+21% y/y) and crossed $1bn in first-half tracked-channel retail sales; [[Rockstar Energy]] — acquired by Celsius in August 2025 — contributed $66.5m, though its tracked-channel retail sales fell 13% y/y. Then Friday, shares reversed to their best single day in a year, up roughly 12%, on news that Russ Savage — the founder who built Rockstar from a mortgaged condo into a $3.85bn PepsiCo acquisition in 2020 — has quietly amassed a 4.7% stake (~12m shares, ~$300m) in Celsius and is publicly demanding the removal of Chairman/CEO John Fieldly, the COO, and two other executives, offering himself as the next CEO. ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4629308-celsius-jumps-on-report-rockstar-energy-founder-takes-stake-wants-to-be-ceo), [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/CELH/celsius-holdings-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-5iep50eehe18.html))

**5.2 Why it matters:** This is a genuinely new, self-contained story — an activist campaign launched by a proven operator in the exact product category, the day after a bad print, against a company that now literally owns the brand its would-be new CEO built and later sold. It's also a live test of how much patience the market has left for a "beat but decelerating" narrative once an alternative leadership path is on the table: the stock's reversal suggests investors are pricing meaningful odds of a shake-up, not just absorbing the earnings miss.

**5.3 What's priced in:** Celsius has given no public signal it will cede control, and PepsiCo — an 8.5% shareholder and Celsius's own distributor — is a complicating stakeholder whose incentives in any leadership change aren't yet clear. Friday's 12% pop suggests the market is assigning real odds to some form of change (a board seat, a settlement, or more), but a drawn-out proxy fight, a poison pill, or an outright stalemate all remain live outcomes the current price doesn't obviously distinguish between.

**5.4 Monitor:** whether Celsius's board responds publicly (engagement, a settlement, or rejection); whether Savage files a formal 13D or launches a proxy contest; how PepsiCo — as both a large shareholder and Celsius's distributor — positions itself; and whether the flagship Celsius brand's underlying sales trend (the -12% y/y print that triggered Thursday's selloff) stabilizes independent of the leadership fight.

## 6. Idea Lab — [[Sweetgreen]] (NYSE: SG)

*Rotation to small/mid-cap consumer discretionary — a first in recent weeks after runs through pharma, shipping and financial conglomerates — prompted by a fast-moving, high-conviction news hook rather than a scheduled earnings catalyst alone.*

**6.1 How it makes money:** Sweetgreen operates fast-casual salad-and-bowl restaurants — 287 locations at Q2's end, of which 35 use its "Infinite Kitchen" automated assembly line — earning revenue almost entirely from company-owned restaurant sales, with no material franchise revenue. Three KPIs matter most: (1) **same-store sales growth**, since Sweetgreen's now-negative comps (-6.2% in Q2, guided to a 7-8% decline for the full year, down from a prior 2-4% decline guide) is the single number determining whether its build-out is adding profitable volume or diluting an already-strained base; (2) **restaurant-level profit margin** (13.1% in Q2), the cleanest read on unit economics before corporate overhead; (3) **digital sales mix** (67.2% of revenue in Q1), because Sweetgreen's operating model — including the Infinite Kitchen automation it's betting its margin recovery on — is built around digital ordering and pickup rather than dine-in traffic.

> [!bullish]
> **6.2 Bull:** Q2's guidance cut is driven by a consumer-fear reaction to a multistate cyclosporiasis outbreak that Sweetgreen says it has no confirmed link to — it doesn't use iceberg lettuce, the ingredient implicated in the outbreak — meaning the stock may be pricing near-panic for a demand hit that proves temporary and unrelated to Sweetgreen's own food safety.

> [!bearish]
> **6.3 Bear:** A ~30% slide since mid-July and a swing from a projected $1-6m adjusted EBITDA profit to a guided $23-27m loss is a lot of financial damage to pin entirely on unproven outbreak fears, and it lands on top of an already-fragile turnaround story with negative comps — real or perceived, the outbreak scare is compounding a demand problem Sweetgreen had before it started.

**6.4 Differentiating question:** Is Sweetgreen's same-store sales decline predominantly a temporary, outbreak-driven demand shock that reverses once the health scare fades from headlines, or a structural erosion of its value proposition against cheaper fast-casual competitors — and which of those two stories would actually change your view on whether the Infinite Kitchen margin bet is worth waiting for?

## 7. Trade Floor — Two Open Tensions

**7.1 FICC/Commodity — Iran says an Oman transit-corridor deal is "very close," and hours later a fresh unidentified-projectile strike hit a cargo vessel off Oman. Is the market underpricing how close a genuine, if partial, de-escalation actually is, or is a rhetorical "close to a deal" claim worth nothing against a live pattern of tanker strikes that hasn't stopped?** One side: Iran's own framing — two separate, sequential gates (an Oman corridor, then full reopening on separate US terms) — is a more concrete diplomatic structure than anything on the table earlier in the war, and even a partial corridor deal would meaningfully de-risk the transit story oil has been pricing all month. Other side: a deal that explicitly excludes reopening the Strait changes little for the actual supply-risk premium, and a fresh strike landing the same day the "very close" framing broke is a reminder that rhetoric and battlefield reality haven't converged even once this month. *Settling datapoint: whether Iran's parliament actually brings the restrictive shipping-corridor bill to a floor vote in the coming week, and whether further UKMTO-flagged incidents follow this weekend's strike or it proves isolated.*

**7.2 Equity — SK Hynix's board just committed $38bn to two new DRAM fabs, and the stock fell on the news. Is the market correctly pricing a 2027-28 memory oversupply risk from an industry-wide capacity race, or is it punishing exactly the kind of long-horizon capital commitment that built SK Hynix's HBM leadership in the first place?** One side: every major memory maker expanding capacity at once, chasing the same AI-driven demand forecast, is the classic setup for a supply glut once the buildout catches up with actual orders — the market has seen this cycle before in memory chips specifically. Other side: SK Hynix's HBM position exists because it committed capital ahead of demand in previous cycles, and a market that punishes every future-facing capex decision risks conflating "more supply" with "the wrong kind of supply" when HBM4-specific capacity may be exactly what next year's AI buildout still needs. *Settling datapoint: Tuesday's Super Micro Computer FQ4 earnings and its commentary on 2027 component-supply visibility, the next concrete read on whether the AI-server complex sees a shortage or a glut coming.*
