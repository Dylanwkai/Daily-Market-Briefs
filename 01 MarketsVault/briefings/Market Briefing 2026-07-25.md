## 2. Theme Tracker

### 2.1 fed-path — US Fed path & inflation — STABLE (holding at an elevated, contested pitch)
- **What changed:** Little moved the numbers themselves day over day — July-hike odds held near 38% Friday versus ~36.5% Thursday, September odds steady near 82% — but the framing hardened. WSJ's Nick Timiraos described the 28-29 July meeting as one of the most unpredictable in years, with Chair Warsh the wildcard because he's stressed price stability repeatedly without signaling a lean either way. Separately, 9 of 18 FOMC members' own dot-plot projections already show a rate above today's level by year-end — a detail that's been sitting in plain sight but is only now getting attention given oil's move.
- **Market effect:** US 10-year yield held at 4.701% Friday (from 4.71% Thursday) — consolidating rather than extending. DXY rose 0.30% Friday, its best week since mid-June, entirely on the rate-differential story.
- **Concept:** A "dot plot" is each FOMC member's individual anonymous projection for where rates should be at future year-ends, plotted together — it's a survey of intentions, not a promise. When 9 of 18 dots already sit above the current rate, the committee's own median view has been quietly hawkish for a while; it just took an external shock (oil) to make the market actually price that possibility instead of assuming it away.

### 2.2 hormuz-oil — Hormuz / Iran war — STRENGTHENING (militarily), but the price backed off
- **What changed:** The war escalated on multiple fronts even as spot oil pulled back. The US ran a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iranian command, drone-storage and coastal-surveillance sites; Iran rejected an Iraqi-PM-brokered ceasefire proposal because it left Hormuz control "unresolved"; and Saturday saw Houthi drones/missiles hit Saudi Aramco's Jizan and Yanbu facilities in retaliation for Friday's Saudi strikes on Hodeidah — a new, direct Saudi-Houthi exchange layered on top of the Encelia/Layla tanker attacks. Lloyd's List's hard transit data confirmed the shutdown is real, not rhetorical: just 53 vessels transited the week to 20 July, down 66% from 157 the prior week, with tanker/gas-carrier crossings falling from 90 to 30 and 469 vessels now anchored or stopped near the strait.
- **Market effect:** Brent fell ~4% Friday to roughly $97, off Thursday's $100.69 peak, on reports that Pakistan is separately brokering US-Iran mediation talks — even though the ceasefire proposal on the table was just rejected. WTI eased to ~$90.47. Oil is still up roughly 8% on the week.
- **Concept:** Spot prices react to the probability-weighted expectation of future supply, not just current damage — so a credible rumor of mediation can pull the price down even while the physical situation on the ground gets worse, because traders are pricing a chance of resolution alongside the chance it fails. That's a riskier setup than it looks: if Monday brings confirmation the talks are dead, Friday's pullback unwinds fast, potentially with a gap higher rather than a grind.

## 5. Deep Dive — A $110bn Merger Gets Paused by a Court, Not by Washington

**5.1 What happened:** Twelve Democratic state attorneys general, led by California's Rob Bonta, sued 13 July in the Northern District of California to block Paramount Skydance's roughly $110bn acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery — a deal the US Department of Justice cleared 12 June and the European Commission cleared 22 July. The states' complaint invokes Clayton Act Section 7, arguing the combination would leave three distributors controlling 75% (four controlling 86%) of wide-release theatrical distribution, give the combined entity over 30% of top-grossing "blockbuster" releases, and merge the #2 (WBD) and #3 (Paramount) national basic-cable channel licensors into a combined ~27% share. On Friday 24 July, Paramount and WBD agreed to a stipulated pause: the merger won't close until five days after a court ruling or 1 June 2027, whichever comes first. US District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín approved it. ([California DOJ](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-files-lawsuit-block-110-billion-warner-brosparamount), [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/paramount-warner-bros-merger-states-lawsuit-1236806824/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/antoniopequenoiv/2026/07/24/paramount-delays-warner-bros-discovery-merger-why-the-decision-could-cost-it-big-time/))

**5.2 Why it matters:** This is a live test of whether individual states can hold up a merger that federal and European regulators have already cleared — a form of antitrust enforcement independent of, and potentially in tension with, the executive branch's own competition authorities. The mechanics carry real teeth: the merger agreement includes a "ticking fee" of $0.00277778 per WBD share per day (capped at $0.25/share per 90-day period, roughly $7m/day or ~$650m/quarter) owed by Paramount to WBD shareholders for every day past 30 September 2026 the deal remains unclosed, on top of a separate $7bn termination fee if the deal collapses outright on regulatory grounds. Running the clock to the stipulated 1 June 2027 outer date could cost Paramount over $1.9bn in ticking fees alone.
- Paramount called the pause "a significant win" because it delivers "a direct path to a trial based on the evidence," and separately called the states' claims "a fundamentally flawed application of the antitrust laws." Bonta called the stipulation "a critical early win."

**5.3 What's priced in:** PSKY fell 3.3% Friday to $8.21, its third consecutive down week, while WBD slipped a milder 0.7% to $25.77 (-4.1% on the week) — a spread that reads as the market pricing real delay risk into Paramount specifically (which bears the ticking-fee cost) while WBD holders still assign meaningful odds the deal eventually closes. Raymond James analyst Ric Prentiss reportedly still expects the deal to close, framing the state/UK-regulatory overhang as a pressure on PSKY rather than a deal-killer.

**5.4 Monitor:** Any scheduling of a preliminary-injunction hearing or trial date before Judge Martínez-Olguín (who also oversees a related Paramount+ subscriber antitrust suit); whether other state AGs join the 12 already suing; and whether this becomes a template other states apply to future large media, tech or telecom mergers regardless of federal clearance.

## 6. Idea Lab — [[Kioxia Holdings]] (TSE: 285A)

*Learning exercise, not a recommendation. Rotation into Japanese NAND flash memory — a mid-cap, under-covered-relative-to-Samsung/SK Hynix name tied directly to this week's AI-capex theme and this weekend's Nvidia Physical AI Coalition news, and a deliberate change of both sector and geography from yesterday's Frontline (tanker shipping) pick.*

**6.1 How it makes money:** Kioxia, spun out of Toshiba Memory and IPO'd in Tokyo in December 2023, is a pure-play NAND flash memory maker — it makes no DRAM and therefore has zero direct HBM exposure, unlike Samsung, SK Hynix or Micron. Its product mix spans enterprise/data-center SSDs, client/consumer SSDs, embedded memory (eMMC/UFS for phones and autos) and memory cards, produced through a long-running fab joint venture with SanDisk that shares capex and R&D across Kioxia's Yokkaichi and Kitakami plants. By Q3 2025 market share, Samsung led at 32.3%, SK Hynix 19.3%, Kioxia 15.3% (having overtaken Micron), SanDisk 12.4%. FY2025 (ended March 2026) revenue hit a record ¥2.34tn (+37% y/y), operating profit ¥870bn (+93% y/y); Q4 FY2025 alone saw operating profit rise 15x y/y. Kioxia's own guidance for Q1 FY2026 (due 31 July, after this briefing) calls for revenue of ¥1.75tn and non-GAAP operating margin near 74%, with net profit guided to jump roughly 48x quarter-on-quarter. Three KPIs matter most: (1) bit shipment growth versus ASP (average selling price) growth — Kioxia's recent surge has come almost entirely from more than doubling ASPs sequentially while bit shipments actually fell, meaning it's a pricing story, not a volume story, which is a different and more fragile kind of upcycle; (2) capex discipline — capex is running near just 5% of sales versus a >20% historical average, though ¥470bn/year is now guided for FY26-28 (+66% versus FY25); (3) hyperscaler long-term supply agreements, which lock in both price and volume ahead of any downturn. ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/01/29/news-second-tier-no-more-kioxia-and-sandisk-balance-alliance-and-rivalry-in-ai-nand-race/), [TrendForce capex](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/06/03/news-kioxia-targets-%C2%A5470b-yearly-capex-in-fy26-28-up-66-from-fy25-reportedly-weighing-third-kitakami-fab-and-ma/))

> [!bullish]
> **6.2 Bull:** Disciplined supply growth (capex still far below its historical share of sales) meeting AI-driven demand has more than doubled NAND ASPs in a single quarter, and this weekend's Nvidia "Physical AI Coalition" signing — which specifically commits Kioxia to co-developing ~100x-faster SSDs with Nvidia for generative-AI servers, targeting a 2027 launch — extends the relationship beyond a one-off pricing cycle into a multi-year design partnership.

> [!bearish]
> **6.3 Bear:** NAND has a long boom-bust history, and Kioxia's stock already fell roughly 50% from its June peak on "AI overheating" fears before this week even started — Friday's Nikkei session named Kioxia among the index's biggest drags, alongside Advantest, just days before its own earnings. A pricing-led (not volume-led) surge is precisely the kind of rally that unwinds fastest once hyperscaler capex growth decelerates even slightly.

**6.4 Differentiating question:** When Kioxia reports Q1 FY2026 on 31 July, does management describe forward hyperscaler commitments as multi-year, price-and-volume-locked agreements (evidence the current ASP surge is becoming structural, reinforced by the Nvidia design-in relationship) — or does the guided 48x sequential profit jump come with any hedge language about pricing normalizing later in the fiscal year? The answer says as much about whether this AI-memory cycle has legs into 2027 as it does about Kioxia specifically.

## 7. Trade Floor — Two Open Tensions

**7.1 Equity — Korea has triggered 41 sidecars and 7 circuit breakers in 2026, more than the entire 2008 financial crisis. Is Kospi's whipsaw a real fundamentals story about AI-chip capex doubt, or has the market's own plumbing (single-stock leveraged ETFs launched in May) become the dominant driver, independent of the news?** One side: the frequency is now so far outside historical norms — more triggers in seven months than in a global financial crisis — that mechanical, forced-selling dynamics from leveraged-ETF rebalancing look like the proximate cause on any given day, not investors reassessing Samsung or SK Hynix's actual earnings power. Other side: Kospi's moves have tracked real news closely enough (Tesla's Thursday crash, Alphabet's capex guidance) that it's hard to wave away the fundamentals entirely — mechanical amplification doesn't mean the underlying direction is wrong. *Settling datapoint: whether Samsung's and SK Hynix's 29-30 July earnings calls (consensus expects DRAM revenue +265% y/y, NAND +330% y/y) trigger a violent Kospi reaction in either direction — a muted reaction despite blowout numbers would point toward market-structure, not fundamentals, as the real driver.*

**7.2 FICC/oil — Brent fell roughly 4% Friday on reports of Pakistan-brokered US-Iran mediation talks, even though Iran had just rejected a separate ceasefire proposal and opened a new front by striking Saudi Aramco facilities. Is the oil market pricing a de-escalation that hasn't actually happened, or is this rational profit-taking after Thursday's 7% spike regardless of the mediation story?** One side: Lloyd's List's hard transit data (53 vessels through Hormuz in the week to 20 July, down 66%) shows the physical disruption is real and getting worse, not better — a price pullback based on an unconfirmed mediation report looks premature and could reverse violently on any negative headline. Other side: Thursday's spike to $100.69 was itself an extreme one-day move, and some mean-reversion after a 7% jump is normal market behavior independent of the war's trajectory — not every price move needs a fundamental cause. *Settling datapoint: the next Lloyd's List/PortWatch Hormuz transit count, and whether the Pakistan-brokered talks produce any confirmed framework versus going silent by mid-week.*

## 11. Journal Seed

The thing most worth forming your own view on this weekend isn't a single data point — it's a divergence. The Iran war got objectively worse (a new Saudi-Houthi front, a rejected ceasefire, hard transit data confirming Hormuz is close to shut), and yet Brent fell 4% Friday on an unconfirmed mediation report.

Is the oil market making a reasonable bet that de-escalation talks are more informative than any single day's violence, or is it simply tired after Thursday's 7% spike and grasping at the first bullish-sounding headline to justify giving some back? What would you need to see confirmed by Monday — a specific transit-data print, a named mediation framework, a fresh strike — to know which one it actually was?
