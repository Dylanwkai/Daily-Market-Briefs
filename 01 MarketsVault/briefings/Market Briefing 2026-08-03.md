## 2. Theme Tracker

### 2.1 fed-path — US Fed path & inflation — TURNING (odds sliding intraday even as growth data runs hot)
- **What changed:** July's ISM Manufacturing PMI printed 55.6%, up 2.3 points from June and the strongest since May 2022 — the seventh straight month of expansion, with New Orders (56.7%) and Production (58.5) both accelerating. That's the kind of number that would normally argue for a more hawkish Fed. Instead, September hike odds slipped to roughly 68% Monday, down from Friday's 81-82%, because Monday's ~5% Brent crash (→2.2) knocked the legs out from under the oil-driven half of the inflation case just as the labor-cost half stayed intact.
- **Market effect:** US 10-year yield eased to around 4.70% from Friday's 4.73-4.75% high; DXY softened slightly to 99.72 (-0.19%).
- **Concept:** The Fed's September call has been hostage to two separate inflation stories that don't have to move together — a genuinely hot labor-cost print, and an oil shock adding cost-push inflation on top of it. Today is that divergence playing out in real time: strong PMI data pulls the "hike" case one way, a falling oil price pulls it the other, and the net move in Fed-odds tells you which story the market currently weighs more heavily. Right now, that's the oil story.

### 2.2 hormuz-oil — Hormuz / Iran war — TURNING (markets made a real bet Monday, on a claim that's still narrower than advertised)
- **What changed:** Trump escalated the rhetoric and the specificity simultaneously. He called Monday's resumed talks Iran's "last chance" before "decapitation," said the US would maintain its naval blockade until "a deal or total surrender," and outlined a two-phase structure — Hormuz reopening first, denuclearization second, the latter taking "a little while." Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei pushed back on the framing within hours, saying Iran's negotiations are with Oman specifically over a Hormuz shipping corridor (one shared route replacing today's separate northern and southern lanes), and that broader US-Iran issues are for "the next stages" — not confirming Trump's characterization of a near-complete bilateral deal.
- **Market effect:** Brent fell 5.24% to $83.32, WTI fell 6.06% to $79.54 — the largest single-session oil move since the weekend's claims first surfaced, and a partial unwind of July's 20%+ surge. US equity futures rose (S&P +0.63%, Nasdaq +0.59%, Dow +0.49%) on the lower energy-cost read-through.
- **Concept:** There's a real difference between a confirmed event and a claimed one, and yesterday's edition flagged that markets hadn't yet chosen sides. Today they did — a 5% single-session move is markets actively pricing optimism into an asset ahead of confirmation, the "buy the rumor" pattern. That's a real position, not a wait-and-see one, which means it's also a real position to lose if Tuesday's session brings a Baqaei-style narrowing rather than the broader deal Trump described.

### 2.3 ai-capex — AI capex cycle — TURNING (Friday's record rally largely mean-reverted within 48 hours)
- **What changed:** Kospi closed down 5.12% at 6,257.45, erasing most of Friday's record 17.91% single-day surge. Samsung Electronics fell roughly 8% to around 241,500 won and SK Hynix fell roughly 8.8% to around 1,567,000 won, both giving back the bulk of Friday's earnings-driven gains. Nikkei fell a milder 1.09% to 63,659.44. The reversal lines up with the leverage-unwind explanation flagged in yesterday's edition — Friday's Kospi surge landed the same day new cash-deposit rules on leveraged ETFs took effect.
- **Market effect:** Regional chip stocks broadly lower; China tech names also caught in the selloff per wire reports. STI dipped modestly (closed around 5,607, -0.38%) on the regional spillover, even as falling oil should be a net tailwind for Singapore's energy-cost-sensitive names.
- **Concept:** A single day's index move can reflect fundamentals, mechanical positioning, or both, and you usually can't separate them until the mechanical flow is exhausted. Monday's reversal doesn't resolve which force dominated Friday's move — it shows both forces can flip direction within 48 hours, which means single-session prints in this sector remain a noisy read on the underlying AI-capex demand thesis rather than a clean one.

### 2.4 bond-regime — Bond market regime — STABLE, easing off Friday's highs
- **What changed:** The 10-year yield eased to around 4.70% from Friday's 4.73-4.75% (a 12-month high) as Monday's oil-price crash reduced near-term inflation risk, pulling against the hawkish signal from a strong ISM print. VIX ticked up slightly to 16.18 (+1.19%) off Friday's multi-week low of 15.99 — still low given three days of volatile Iran headlines. Gold ticked up to $4,111.60 (+0.11%); Bitcoin held near $62,700, roughly flat.
- **Market effect:** A modest bond rally, a slightly softer dollar, oil down sharply — a cross-asset pattern consistent with markets reading today's news as net disinflationary.
- **Concept:** The 10-year yield synthesizes competing signals rather than reflecting just one — strong growth data pulls it up, falling energy prices pull it down. Today's net "easing" tells you which signal is currently winning that tug-of-war in the bond market's read, and it's the same signal driving the Fed-odds move in 2.1: right now, the oil story is dominating the growth story.

## 5. Deep Dive — SpaceX Trades Below Its IPO Price Ahead of Tuesday's First-Ever Public Earnings

**5.1 What happened:** SpaceX went public June 12, 2026, at $135 a share — the largest IPO in history, raising $75-85.7bn and valuing the company near $1.8 trillion. The stock closed Day 1 at $160.95 (+19%, briefly a $2.1 trillion market cap), peaked intraday at $225.64 on June 16, then fell three straight sessions. As of Monday, [[SpaceX]] trades around $107 — below even its IPO price and near a fresh 52-week low. Tuesday, August 4 (earnings call 4:30pm ET, audio-only webcast) is the company's first-ever quarterly report as a public company, breaking out results across three newly disclosed segments: Space (launch), Connectivity (Starlink), and AI (SpaceXAI). Consensus expects Q2 revenue of roughly $6.72-6.82bn, up from Q1's $4.7bn, and a loss of about $0.26 a share. The same week brings SpaceX's first insider lockup expiration. ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/03/spacex-reports-its-first-ever-earnings-tuesday-her/), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/one-week-post-ipo-heres-151558434.html), [Zacks](https://zacks.com/stock/news/2966351/stock-market-news-for-aug-3-2026))

**5.2 Why it matters:** Five numbers will do most of the work of validating or undermining the IPO valuation. (1) Total revenue vs. consensus, with particular attention on how much of the beat (or miss) comes from the newer AI compute business versus legacy Space and Connectivity. (2) Starlink's average revenue per user, which has fallen from roughly $99/month in 2023 to about $66/month in Q1 2026 — continued erosion would be a real demand-quality concern for the segment carrying the rest of the company. (3) Whether SpaceXAI's claimed $28.5 trillion addressable market translates into more contracts like its existing roughly $920m-a-month Google Cloud deal, or stays a single-customer story. (4) Connectivity segment margin, since Starlink is described as the company's only consistently profitable business and needs to keep offsetting losses in Space and AI. (5) Full-year guidance — SpaceX has never given financial guidance before, so Tuesday's framework (or lack of one) will itself move the stock regardless of the quarter's actual numbers.

**5.3 What's priced in:** Unlike most hot-IPO first earnings, where a story stock still has a premium to defend, SpaceX has already fallen roughly 50% from its June peak and now sits below the price at which it originally sold shares to the public. That's the market pricing in real skepticism ahead of the print — a materially different setup from one where disappointment has yet to be tested.

**5.4 Monitor:** Tuesday's actual segment-level revenue split and whether management issues any form of full-year guidance; the Starlink ARPU trend specifically; whether the insider lockup expiration adds selling pressure independent of the earnings reaction; and whether the AI segment's disclosures offer any read-through to the broader AI-capex debate already playing out in Kospi and Nikkei (→2.3).

## 6. Idea Lab — [[Singapore Airlines]] (SGX: C6L)

*Rotation to Singapore's own flag carrier — a home-market blue chip directly exposed to today's single biggest market move (the Brent crash), and a useful counterpoint to recent Idea Lab picks in EVs, semi-equipment, memory, and tanker shipping.*

**6.1 How it makes money:** SIA operates two brands — full-service SIA mainline (long-haul, premium-skewed) and budget carrier Scoot — earning through a mix of capacity deployed (available seat-kilometres), load factor (how much of that capacity sells), and yield (revenue per revenue-passenger-kilometre), net of fuel costs, which are the single largest and most volatile expense line for any airline. Q1 FY2026/27 (April-June 2026): record group revenue of roughly $5.7bn (+19.3% YoY), but the group swung to a net loss of about $76m as gross fuel cost before hedging surged 118.7% amid the Hormuz war — the jet-fuel-price effect alone adding roughly $1,459m in costs. A fuel-hedging gain of $376m (versus a $60m hedging loss the year before) partially cushioned the blow. Passenger load factor came in at 87.1% (-0.5pp YoY, as capacity growth of 5.9% outpaced traffic growth of 5.3%), while yields rose 12% to 11.2 cents per RPK. Three KPIs that matter most: (1) **passenger yield** — pricing power relative to capacity growth; (2) **load factor** — whether demand is actually absorbing that added capacity; (3) **fuel cost net of hedging gains as a share of operating expenses** — the single biggest lever on the P&L in a war-disrupted oil market, and the one most directly tied to today's Brent move. ([Travel Weekly](https://travelweekly.com.au/sia-posts-au84m-q1-loss-on-fuel-costs-expands-australian-network/), [Singapore Airlines investor relations](https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/ca/corporate/newsroom/press-release/2026/july---september-2026/NR-0326/))

> [!bullish]
> **6.2 Bull:** If today's ~5% Brent crash holds and extends as the Hormuz talks progress, SIA's single largest and most volatile cost line falls sharply just as its yields and record revenue trajectory continue — setting up a swing back to profitability without SIA changing anything operationally.

> [!bearish]
> **6.3 Bear:** The fuel-hedging book that just cushioned a $76m loss into something survivable was locked in before this specific war-driven cost spike; if oil reverses again — and Iran's Baqaei is still only confirming a narrow Hormuz-corridor discussion, not the broader deal Trump described — SIA's hedges may not protect the next quarter as well, and capacity already growing faster than traffic leaves less room to absorb a second shock.

**6.4 Differentiating question:** How much of SIA's current fuel-hedge book is still marked at pre-crisis, war-era pricing versus rolling off at now-lower spot prices? That determines whether today's oil crash shows up as a real P&L tailwind next quarter, or gets absorbed by hedges that locked in the worst of the war and now trade against the company.

Other side: a 5-9% single-session reversal in the region's most AI-exposed names, on no negative company-specific news, is also consistent with a market that overpaid for Friday's optimism and is now recalibrating toward the more cautious end of the AI-capex debate that's been simmering for weeks. *Settling datapoint: whether Tuesday's Kospi session stabilizes or extends the slide, and whether SpaceX's AI-segment disclosure (→5) adds a fresh, independent data point to the broader capex-demand debate.*
