## 2. Theme Tracker

### 2.1 fed-path — US Fed path & inflation — TURNING (dovish reversal, second flip in 48 hours)
- **What changed:** July nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 against roughly an 83,000 gain expected, with sizeable downward revisions to the prior two months' prints; unemployment held near 4.1%. This followed Thursday's ADP miss (44,000 vs. ~70-75,000 est.) and a services PMI miss (54.1 vs. 55.1 est.) — three straight soft data points in a row. CME FedWatch's implied probability of a 25bp September hike collapsed to 44%, down from 58.3% Thursday and 74.5% Wednesday.
- **Market effect:** The 2-year Treasury yield fell 8bp to 4.16%, the 10-year fell 6bp to 4.61% — a bull-steepening move. The dollar index slid to roughly 99.4, its softest level in two months; the yen rallied toward 157.20 from near 159. Asian equities mostly read it as good news for risk — Hang Seng +0.54%, STI +1.05% — though the Kospi still slipped 0.60%, still working through the memory-chip air pocket (→2.3).
- **Concept:** Front-end yields (2-year) track near-term policy expectations almost mechanically — they move roughly one-for-one with the market's Fed-meeting-by-meeting rate path. Long-end yields (10-year) track a blend of growth and inflation expectations further out, so they move less on any single data point. That's why the 2-year fell more than the 10-year on this miss — a genuine "bull steepening" of the curve, the classic signature of a market repricing near-term policy rather than the long-run economic outlook.

### 2.2 hormuz-oil — Hormuz / Iran war — TURNING (military risk up, price impact overridden by demand fears)
- **What changed:** Iran's armed forces reportedly struck "hostile enemy targets" near Qeshm Island in Hormozgan province Thursday night, per Iran's semi-official Tasnim news agency, even as the Iran-Oman shipping-corridor draft — the same restrictive terms flagged Wednesday, barring US and Israeli vessels and fining violators a fifth of cargo value — remains stuck in Iran's parliament. President Trump, who called off a planned US strike earlier in the week, said the conflict "can't go on much longer."
- **Market effect:** Oil fell anyway Friday morning: Brent -0.78% to $81.85, WTI -0.52% to $76.89, as the weak jobs data introduced a demand-side offset to the ongoing supply-side risk premium — the first session all week where the growth read outweighed the geopolitical one.
- **Concept:** Oil prices net two forces simultaneously — a supply-risk premium (how likely is a Hormuz disruption) and a demand-growth read (how much crude the world economy will actually consume). This week showed both forces pulling in opposite directions on different days: Wednesday's restrictive draft terms pushed the supply-risk premium above the demand read; Friday's weak jobs data flipped the emphasis back toward demand, pulling oil down even with the Qeshm Island incident still unresolved. Which force dominates on a given day tells you what the desk is actually pricing that session — it's rarely both at once.

### 2.3 ai-capex — AI capex cycle — STRENGTHENING as a source of dispersion (splitting into winners and skeptics)
- **What changed:** Two starkly different earnings reactions crystallized the split running through this theme. Shopify's Q2 print — revenue +34% to $3.58bn, GMV +32% to $115.6bn, Q3 guidance of low-30s% growth against 26.3% consensus — credited AI-driven order growth (management says AI-driven orders are "tripling") and shares surged as much as 26% intraday, closing +17% at $144.24. That's the mirror image of Wednesday-Thursday's memory-chip air pocket (SanDisk, Western Digital, AppLovin), which kept rippling into Asia Friday: SK Hynix fell as much as 5% intraday before paring losses, Samsung held a 1.5% gain, and the Kospi slipped 0.60% to 6,258.77 despite opening more than 1% higher.
- **Market effect:** The Nasdaq fell 0.8% Thursday on "weak AI giants" even with Shopify's pop; the Kospi gave back an early rally Friday on the same underlying tension.
- **Concept:** The market isn't repricing "AI" as a single trade anymore — it's separating AI demand-side beneficiaries (companies using AI to grow their own revenue, like Shopify) from AI supply-side infrastructure providers (chipmakers and memory manufacturers priced for the capex boom itself to keep accelerating). A name in the first bucket can beat and rally in a week when the second bucket is under a magnifying glass, because the market is asking each a different question: "is AI making you more money" versus "is the AI capex spend itself sustainable."

### 2.4 bond-regime — Bond market regime — TURNING (inflation-scare narrative flips to growth-scare narrative)
- **What changed:** VIX collapsed to 14.90 Thursday (from Wednesday's 20.66) even as gold extended its rally to +2.33% Thursday and further to roughly $4,350 (+2.6%) Friday morning — an unusual pairing, since falling volatility normally coincides with a cooling, not accelerating, gold bid. Friday's weak jobs data resolved some of that tension: the bull-steepening in Treasuries and the dollar's slide to ~99.4 show gold's rally now tracking a coherent dovish-Fed-repricing trade rather than a pure fear gauge.
- **Market effect:** Bitcoin joined the same move, rising to roughly $65,100 (+1.3%), alongside the broader Friday risk-on tone in Asia.
- **Concept:** Tuesday through Thursday, this week's cross-asset moves ran on an inflation-scare narrative — Hormuz oil risk pushing rate-hike odds and yields up together. Friday flipped the underlying story to a growth-scare one — weak jobs data pushing rate-hike odds and yields down together, with the dollar following. Same variables moving, same direction logic each time, but the narrative connecting them reversed entirely within 48 hours — a reminder that headline direction alone doesn't tell you what's being priced; you need to know which fear is currently driving the desk.

## 5. Deep Dive — Shopify's AI-Driven Blowout Is the Week's Cleanest Counter-Example to the Memory-Chip Skeptic Story

**5.1 What happened:** Shopify reported Q2 revenue of $3.58bn, up 34% year-on-year and above the $3.45bn consensus, with gross merchandise volume up 32% to $115.57bn and gross profit up 31% to $1.71bn. Adjusted EPS of $0.42 beat the $0.40 estimate, and free cash flow came in at $654m, an 18% margin. Management said AI-driven orders are "tripling" and that new buyers are arriving nearly twice as fast, and guided Q3 revenue to grow at a low-30s% rate against a 26.3% Street estimate. Shares jumped as much as 26% in premarket trading and closed the session up 17% at $144.24, from around $122 the day before. The print landed the same week SanDisk, Western Digital and AppLovin all reported strong-but-decelerating results and were punished for it (→2.3, prior briefing), and the same day the memory-chip contagion reached Samsung and SK Hynix in Asia. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/shopify-q2-2026-earnings-beat-121421648.html), [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/earnings/26/08/60957108/shopify-says-ai-driven-orders-are-tripling-as-new-buyers-arrive-nearly-twice-as-fast))

**5.2 Why it matters:** The market has spent this week asking "does AI capex actually pay off," and Shopify is the first name this week where the answer came back a clean "yes" rather than "prove it more." That's a genuinely different business model from the names that got punished — an AI demand-side beneficiary (using AI to grow its own order volume and GMV) rather than an AI supply-side infrastructure provider (selling the hardware and memory the boom runs on). That distinction is emerging as the market's real dividing line this week, more useful than treating "AI-exposed" as a single undifferentiated bucket.

**5.3 What's priced in:** Shopify had already been a strong 2026 performer heading into the print, so a 17-26% single-day move on a beat-and-raise suggests the market wasn't fully pricing this scale of AI-driven order acceleration. That's the opposite dynamic from SanDisk, whose roughly 500% year-to-date rally already assumed continued outsized growth — which is exactly why even a solid guide read there as a disappointment (→2.3, prior briefing).

**5.4 Monitor:** Whether other AI-software and e-commerce names further along the "who actually benefits from AI working" spectrum get a similar re-rating in coming earnings; whether the memory-chip complex (Samsung, SK Hynix) stabilizes or the skepticism broadens; and whether next week's Super Micro Computer print (Tue 11 Aug, →9.2) lands in the "punished infrastructure" bucket or breaks the pattern.

**(c) Cross-asset signal of the week — gold rallying as volatility fell, until Friday's data resolved why:** From Wednesday to Thursday, gold rose while the VIX fell sharply (20.66 to 14.90) — an unusual pairing, since gold's safe-haven bid and equity-market fear normally move together. Friday's weak jobs data resolved the puzzle: gold's rally was never purely a fear trade, it was a dovish-Fed-repricing trade, and it kept extending (to roughly $4,350, +2.6%) even as the VIX stayed low and equities took the data as good news. The concept: gold has two separate demand drivers that can point the same direction for different reasons — a fear/uncertainty bid (which tracks the VIX) and a falling-real-yields bid (which tracks rate-cut or rate-hold expectations, since gold pays no interest and becomes relatively more attractive as the opportunity cost of holding it falls). This week showed the second driver taking over from the first — worth watching whether gold keeps climbing on the rate-path story alone even if volatility stays subdued into next week's CPI print (→9.1).

## 7. Idea Lab — [[Eli Lilly]] (NYSE: LLY)

*Rotation to pharma — a first in recent weeks after runs through financial holding companies, shipping, airlines, semis and storage hardware — prompted by the standout beat-and-raise in a week where most beats have been punished.*

**7.1 How it makes money:** Eli Lilly's growth is now dominated by its GLP-1 incretin franchise — Mounjaro (tirzepatide, for diabetes) and Zepbound (tirzepatide, for obesity) — alongside a broader portfolio spanning diabetes, oncology, immunology and neuroscience. Three KPIs matter most: (1) **volume growth in the incretin franchise** — Mounjaro/Zepbound combined revenue hit $14.9bn this quarter (Mounjaro +91% to $9.9bn, Zepbound +44% to $4.9bn in the US alone) — since demand still exceeds Lilly's manufacturing capacity; (2) **manufacturing capacity build-out**, because Lilly's near-term growth ceiling is literally how many vials and pens it can produce, not demand; (3) **international mix and pricing**, since international Mounjaro sales ($5.2bn) are now larger than the US leg, and access/pricing negotiations abroad determine how much of that volume converts to margin.

> [!bullish]
> **7.2 Bull:** Lilly has first-mover scale in the highest-demand drug category in a generation, with volume — not price — driving most of this quarter's growth, and it just raised full-year guidance to $85-87bn, meaning the growth story is still accelerating rather than decelerating, unlike this week's AI-capex disappointments.

> [!bearish]
> **7.3 Bear:** A single drug class now drives the overwhelming majority of Lilly's growth, concentrating the stock's fortunes on continued double-digit volume growth, flawless manufacturing execution, and durable pricing in a category attracting well-funded competitors (Novo Nordisk, and a widening field of oral and next-generation entrants) — a concentration risk not unlike the kind the market punished elsewhere this week.

**7.4 Differentiating question:** How much of Lilly's runway depends on staying ahead specifically on manufacturing capacity (a hard, capital-intensive moat) versus on tirzepatide simply being the best molecule available today (a moat that erodes as next-generation oral GLP-1s and competitor drugs reach the market) — and which of those two answers would actually change your view on the stock two years out?

## 8. Trade Floor — Two Open Tensions

**8.1 FICC/Rates — Fed hike odds cratered from 74.5% Wednesday to 44% Friday on one jobs report. Is this the start of a durable dovish reset, or another headline-driven overreaction that snaps back on the next print?** One side: three consecutive soft data points (ADP, services PMI, NFP) in a single week is a pattern, not noise, and large downward revisions to the prior two months suggest the labor market has been weaker for longer than realized — a trend that doesn't typically reverse in one report. Other side: this week already proved data can whipsaw the narrative twice in 48 hours (an oil-driven hawkish Wednesday, a jobs-driven dovish Friday), and a single monthly payrolls print is itself volatile and heavily revision-prone, so one weak month doesn't yet prove a trend. *Settling datapoint: 12 August's US CPI print — a hot core reading would force the Fed to weigh a cooling labor market against still-sticky inflation, reopening the debate this week just closed.*

**8.2 Equity — Shopify's AI-driven beat rallied 17-26% the same week SanDisk, Western Digital and AppLovin all sold off on "beat but decelerating" guidance. Is the market drawing a genuine, durable line between AI demand-side beneficiaries and AI infrastructure providers, or is Shopify simply a company-specific story that happens to mention AI?** One side: the split is logically coherent — a company using AI to grow its own revenue faces a much lower bar than one whose stock is priced on an entire industry's capex spending accelerating indefinitely, so the divergence should persist as more "AI-user" companies report. Other side: markets have a track record of over-extrapolating one good quarter into a durable framework, and Shopify's own guide could be the next one that "beats but decelerates" once this quarter's unusually high growth base normalizes. *Settling datapoint: how the market treats the next wave of software and e-commerce earnings later in August that cite AI as a growth driver — a repeat Shopify-style re-rating would confirm the split; a punished guide would suggest it was idiosyncratic.*
