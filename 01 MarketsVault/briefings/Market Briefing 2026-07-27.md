- **Concept:** Fed-funds futures continuously price a probability across every upcoming meeting, updated in real time as new information lands — they don't wait for the FOMC to speak. When the underlying driver of a repricing (oil) itself reverses, the futures market usually catches up within a session or two. The gap between "the reason odds rose is gone" and "the odds have actually fallen" is exactly the kind of stale-consensus trap worth watching at Tuesday's NY open.

The 58-of-58 vessel figure matters more than the rhetoric because it's revealed preference — every commercial shipowner making the call chose Iran's route over the contested one, which tells you more about who actually controls Hormuz right now than any diplomatic statement does.

## 5. Deep Dive — China's Homegrown DUV Lithography Machines Wipe $44bn Off [[ASML]]

**5.1 What happened:** The Information reported Sunday that Chinese toolmakers — led by Shanghai Yuliangsheng, a startup linked to Huawei and chip-equipment firm SiCarrier — have begun limited production of homegrown immersion DUV (deep ultraviolet) lithography machines, with roughly five units expected in 2026 and about twenty in 2027. First deliveries are slated for this year to [[SMIC]], [[Hua Hong Semiconductor]] and [[CXMT]]. ASML shares fell 6-7% Monday, their worst single session since June, erasing roughly $44bn of market value; Applied Materials, Lam Research and KLA fell 5-7% in sympathy. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/asml-slides-after-report-of-china-beginning-duv-tool-production), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/china-begins-mass-production-of-domestic-immersion-duv-lithography-machines))

**5.2 Why it matters:** Immersion DUV is the workhorse lithography tool behind most non-cutting-edge logic and memory chips — not the extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) machines used for the most advanced nodes, which remain firmly ASML's monopoly and are barred from export to China entirely. After EUV export controls tightened, Chinese fabs became heavily reliant on stockpiling ASML's older DUV immersion tools to keep expanding capacity — DUV sales had become one of ASML's most resilient China revenue lines precisely because there was no domestic alternative. A credible domestic DUV source, even a generation behind on throughput, overlay accuracy and reliability, threatens that revenue line and — more importantly — threatens the precedent that export controls can permanently wall off a technology layer China wants badly enough. ([Invezz](https://invezz.com/in/news/2026/07/27/asml-stock-sinks-as-china-reportedly-starts-producing-homegrown-duv-chip-machines/))

**5.3 What's priced in:** The $44bn market-cap decline is more than four times ASML's implied 2026 China revenue — a reaction sized for a structural threat, not a near-term sales hit from five machines. That gap is the tell: the market is pricing the erosion of the moat itself (the assumption that the West can indefinitely gate China out of a chip-toolmaking layer), not this year's numbers. The counter-case, still underpriced by some accounts: China's domestic tools reportedly remain a generation behind ASML on the metrics that matter for high-yield production, and five units in 2026 is a rounding error against ASML's installed base and order book. ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/asml-and-us-chip-stocks-sink-on-report-of-chinas-duv-breakthrough-4814319))

**5.4 Monitor:** Actual 2026-2027 delivery timelines to SMIC, Hua Hong and CXMT, and whether they slip; independent yield/reliability data once these tools enter production (as opposed to vendor claims); any Dutch government or US Commerce Department response — this is exactly the kind of development that could trigger a further tightening of DUV-specific export rules; and ASML's own China-revenue commentary on its next earnings call.

## 6. Idea Lab — [[DBS Group Holdings]] (SGX: D05)

*Learning exercise, not a recommendation. Rotation into Singapore banking — the sector at the center of today's dominant local story (MAS's surprise tightening) and a deliberate change from yesterday's UK specialty-insurance pick. DBS became the first SGX-listed company to cross S$200bn market cap on Friday, and today's MAS decision is a live test of a mechanism worth understanding: whether SGD monetary policy actually moves a Singapore bank's earnings the way Fed policy moves a US bank's.*

**6.1 How it makes money:** DBS runs three main segments: Consumer Banking/Wealth Management (deposits, mortgages, cards, and — increasingly — fee-driven wealth management; wealth management income alone rose 9% to SGD 5.7bn in 2025 on record net-new-money inflows), Institutional Banking (lending, cash management and trade finance to corporates, financial institutions and government-linked companies), and Treasury Markets (trading and structuring across rates, FX and credit). FY2025 (reported February 2026): total income hit a record SGD 22.9bn (+3%), profit before tax a record SGD 13.1bn, net profit a record SGD 11.4bn (+11%), and return on equity of 18.0% — matching the prior year's record despite net interest margin (NIM, the spread between what a bank earns on loans/assets and pays on deposits/funding) narrowing to roughly 2.01% as SGD and HKD benchmark rates declined through 2025. Q1 2026 showed NIM ticking back up to 2.12% with ROE still at 18.0% and a CET1 capital ratio (a core measure of a bank's loss-absorbing capital buffer) of 17.2%. Full-year dividends came to $3.06/share, $8.68bn in aggregate — up 38% year-on-year, reflecting an active capital-return program. Three KPIs matter most for reading DBS quarter to quarter: (1) NIM and its direction, since it's the single biggest swing factor in net interest income; (2) wealth management fee income and net-new-money inflows, the highest-growth, capital-light part of the business; and (3) the CET1 ratio, since it determines how much room DBS has to keep raising dividends and buying back stock. ([DBS FY2025 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_full_year_net_profit_up_11pct_to_new_high_of_SGD_11_4_billion_return_on_equity_of_18_0pct_sustained_at_previous_years_record), [DBS 4Q25 CFO presentation](https://www.dbs.com/iwov-resources/images/investors/quarterly-financials/2025/4Q25_CFO_presentation.pdf))

> [!bullish]
> **6.2 Bull:** Record ROE (18%) and a dividend that grew 38% in a single year show DBS can keep compounding capital returns through a rate cycle by leaning on wealth management fee growth — a capital-light, less rate-sensitive income stream — even as the pure net-interest-margin tailwind fades.

> [!bearish]
> **6.3 Bear:** NIM has already compressed from its 2024 peak as SGD and HKD benchmark rates have declined, wealth fee income is inherently markets-dependent and would reverse hard in a genuine risk-off shock, and a record capital-return payout leaves a thinner buffer if credit costs rise from here.

**6.4 Differentiating question:** MAS just delivered a hawkish surprise using a tool — the S$NEER exchange-rate band — that doesn't set a domestic policy interest rate the way the Fed or ECB does; Singapore's actual benchmark lending rate, SORA, tracks global USD liquidity and rate expectations far more than it tracks MAS's band settings. So does today's "hawkish MAS" headline actually change DBS's funding costs and NIM trajectory at all, or is the market instinctively pattern-matching a currency-policy tightening to an interest-rate tightening when the transmission mechanism is genuinely different? Pulling SORA's actual move (if any) over the next few sessions is the concrete way to check which story is right.

## 11. Journal Seed

The thing most worth forming your own view on today is the ASML sell-off, because the reaction size tells you more than the news itself: a $44bn market-cap decline against five machines shipping in 2026 is the market pricing a structural threat to the West's chip-equipment moat, not a 2026 earnings hit. Before ASML's next earnings call, try writing down what you think the actual probability is that Chinese domestic DUV tools reach ASML-comparable yield and reliability within three years — then ask what evidence (delivery counts, fab yield data, ASML's own China order book) would move that number, and watch for it directly rather than trading the headline.
