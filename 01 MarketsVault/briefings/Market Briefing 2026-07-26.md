## 5. Deep Dive — CXMT's Record $85bn Shanghai IPO Turns China's DRAM Ambitions Into a Priced Fact

**5.1 What happened:** CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies), China's largest DRAM maker, listed on Shanghai's STAR Market Monday 27 July, pricing at 8.66 yuan/share and selling roughly 6.7bn shares (10% of enlarged capital) for gross proceeds of about 57.9bn yuan (~$8.5bn) — implying a listing valuation of roughly 579bn yuan (~$85bn). That makes it the largest China A-share chip IPO on record and Asia's biggest IPO of 2026, surpassing SMIC's 2020 debut. Institutional demand ran roughly 570x subscription, retail around 212x, though sentiment was dented relative to prior STAR Market mega-deals by the broader global chip-stock selloff. Specific cornerstone-investor names weren't confirmed in press available at filing time. ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3360615/china-memory-giant-cxmt-valued-us85-billion-record-shanghai-ipo), [SCMP oversubscription](https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3360892/chinese-memory-giant-cxmt-oversubscribed-212-times-mega-shanghai-ipo))

**5.2 Why it matters:** CXMT makes commodity DDR5/LPDDR5X DRAM on a 16nm-class process — roughly 18-24 months behind Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron's leading edge, narrowed from a two-generation gap in 2023. It's now the world's #4 DRAM maker, with global share that's grown from roughly 3% to 8% (China's total DRAM share now above 10%), against Samsung's 38.6%, SK Hynix's 28.8% and Micron's 22.4%. Capacity is scaling fast: from around 40,000 wafers/month a few years ago toward a projected 350,000-375,000 wafers/month by end-2026 — converging with Micron's total capacity. On export controls, CXMT is notably *not* on the formal US Entity List (a May 2025 BIS proposal to add it was reportedly shelved, and it was actually removed from the Pentagon's restricted-companies list), though it remains barred from US Department of Defense business and faces Commerce Department equipment restrictions that block EUV lithography access. On HBM specifically — the high-bandwidth memory that actually feeds AI servers — CXMT is targeting HBM2E as a 2026 bridge product, HBM3 mass production by end-2026 at roughly 20% of capacity, and 12-layer HBM/HBM3E by 2027. Consensus analyst framing: a real and rapidly closing threat in commodity DRAM, but still 2-3 years behind in AI-grade memory. ([DigiTimes](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20251126PD231/dram-cxmt-ddr5-samsung-sk-hynix.html), [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/chinas-cxmt-is-set-to-challenge-dram), [wccftech](https://wccftech.com/cxmt-ymtc-removed-from-pentagon-list-opening-door-for-chinese-dram-adoption/))

**5.3 What's priced in:** The competitive-threat narrative is already old news to the market by the time the IPO actually happened — Micron fell 5% and SK Hynix/SanDisk fell 7% on 16 July specifically on CXMT-IPO/oversupply fears, and Samsung Securities traders cited CXMT rotation as one factor in Friday's 5.7-6.1% Kospi plunge alongside Middle East tensions and leveraged-ETF unwinding. Some sell-side voices (Seoul Economic Daily) argue the threat is overstated given the multi-year HBM gap; Morgan Stanley separately estimates China could supply 30% of new global DRAM capacity by 2028 — a supply-growth number that matters most for commodity DRAM pricing, not for the AI-memory segment currently driving Samsung's and SK Hynix's earnings re-rating. ([247WallSt](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/07/16/sk-hynix-and-sandisk-sink-7-micron-falls-5-as-chinas-cxmt-readies-an-8-6b-memory-ipo/), [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/international/2026/07/09/chinas-cxmt-nears-ipo-but-threat-to-samsung-sk-hynix-may-be))

**5.4 Monitor:** CXMT's HBM3 mass-production progress against its end-2026 target; post-IPO capex and capacity disclosures now that it faces public reporting requirements; any change in US Entity List status; and whether commodity DRAM price pressure from CXMT's capacity ramp spreads into a broader 2027 oversupply story that would hit blended margins even for HBM leaders.

## 6. Idea Lab — [[Beazley plc]] (LSE: BEZ)

*Learning exercise, not a recommendation. Chosen for topicality — Beazley is a Lloyd's of London marine war-risk underwriter directly exposed to the Hormuz disruption dominating this weekend's Theme Tracker — but the real lesson here is different: Beazley is currently the target of an announced takeover, which changes what actually drives the stock. Rotation into UK-listed specialty insurance, a sector not yet covered this week.*

**6.1 How it makes money:** Beazley underwrites specialty insurance through Lloyd's of London syndicates, organized into five divisions: Specialty (executive risk/management liability, ~$1.98bn of FY2025's ~$6.10bn insurance written premium), Property (~$1.73bn, including reinsurance), Cyber (~$1.16bn — its single largest named segment), MAP (Marine, Aviation, Political, Accident, Contingency, ~$1.00bn) and Digital (~$0.23bn). Marine war risk sits inside MAP, and in April 2026 Beazley launched a $1bn marine war consortium ($500m hull war, $500m cargo war) specifically to underwrite Hormuz transits — real, live exposure to this week's dominant theme, even though MAP is only around 16% of the total book. The three KPIs that matter most for a specialty Lloyd's insurer: (1) combined ratio — the sum of claims paid plus expenses as a share of premium, where under 100% means underwriting profit; Beazley's FY2025 undiscounted combined ratio was 81.2% (FY2024: 79.0%), with H1 2025 at 84.9% (H1 2024: 80.7%) as California wildfire catastrophe losses hit; (2) gross written premium growth by division, since a shrinking or growing book signals where pricing power is moving; and (3) investment return on the float — the premium collected but not yet paid out in claims — which added over $300m in H1 2025 at a 2.7% return. FY2025 profit before tax was $1.15bn, a third consecutive $1bn+ year, though down year-on-year. **Critical context that changes everything below:** Beazley is subject to a recommended £8.1bn cash takeover by Zurich Insurance Group, announced 2 March 2026 at 1,335p/share (1,310p cash plus a 25p permitted dividend) — a 62.8% premium to the undisturbed price. Shareholders approved it 99.9% in April; the EU cleared it 7 July; Australia cleared it in June. What's left is UK regulatory sign-off (PRA, FCA, Lloyd's) plus Swiss (FINMA) approval and a UK court sanction hearing, with completion targeted for H2 2026. The stock now trades primarily on deal-completion probability, not on Beazley's standalone fundamentals. ([Beazley Interim Report 2025](https://www.beazley.com/globalassets/ir-documents/interim-reports/interim-report-2025/interim_report_2025.pdf), [Zurich Rule 2.7 announcement](https://www.zurich.com/media/news-releases/2026/2026-0302-01), [Insurance Journal — Beazley $1bn marine war consortium](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2026/04/17/866350.htm))

> [!bullish]
> **6.2 Bull:** The deal closes on schedule in H2 2026, locking in the 1,335p cash payout regardless of what happens to the underlying business — and if it somehow broke, holders would fall back on a franchise with a three-year, $1bn+ profit track record and a marine war-risk book (April's $1bn Hormuz consortium) positioned for exactly the disruption now dominating headlines.

> [!bearish]
> **6.3 Bear:** Real, if narrowing, deal-break risk remains in the final regulatory gate (PRA, FCA, Lloyd's, FINMA, plus a UK court sanction hearing) with completion only "targeted" for H2 2026 — and if it broke, holders get re-exposed to Beazley's largest segment, cyber, which is mid-deteriorating-cycle: H1 2025 cyber rates fell 6.8%, nine-month cyber premium fell 8% to $848m, and Beazley's own CEO has called the US cyber market "unprofitable."

**6.4 Differentiating question:** With only UK/Swiss regulatory sign-off and a court sanction hearing left before a targeted H2 2026 close, is Beazley's live trading spread to the 1,335p offer still wide enough to compensate for the residual approval-timing risk — or has the market already priced this as a near-certainty, in which case the "trade" here isn't Beazley at all but whatever a specialty-insurance investor rotates the freed-up capital into next? Answering it requires actually pulling Beazley's live quote against 1,335p — a concrete lesson in reading a merger-arb spread as a probability estimate rather than a stock price.

## 7. Trade Floor — Two Open Tensions

**7.1 Equity — CXMT is now a public, priced fact. Is Korea's chip complex being repriced for a genuine structural threat, or was CXMT's IPO simply the proximate excuse for money already rotating out of a crowded, mechanically-levered Kospi trade?** One side: CXMT's capacity scaling (350,000-375,000 wafers/month by end-2026, converging with Micron) and global DRAM share climbing from 3% to 8% is a real structural shift in commodity memory, and Samsung's and SK Hynix's re-rating has run almost entirely on AI-driven HBM pricing — so any erosion in the commodity-DRAM half of their businesses is a legitimate reason to trim exposure. Other side: CXMT remains 2-3 years behind on HBM specifically, the segment actually driving Korea's AI-era earnings — rotating out of Samsung/SK Hynix into a commodity-DRAM-only Chinese IPO is a mismatched swap unless you specifically expect commodity DRAM weakness to outrun HBM growth in the blended margin. *Settling datapoint: Samsung's and SK Hynix's 29-30 July earnings calls — specifically their HBM revenue mix disclosure and any 2027 guidance language on commodity DRAM ASPs — will show whether the two businesses are as separable as the bull case on Korea memory claims.*

**7.2 FICC/oil — Is the US-Iran mutual strike pause and the Oman-brokered Hormuz-transit compromise a real off-ramp, or the same "rumor of mediation" pattern that pulled Brent down 4% Friday only for the war to keep escalating underneath it?** One side: a reciprocal two-day pause plus a specific new proposal (Iran managing its own transit with fewer restrictions) is qualitatively different from the Iraqi-brokered ceasefire Iran rejected Thursday — it actually addresses the point of contention (who controls Hormuz) instead of talking past it. Other side: Trump remains rhetorically "locked and loaded," the US naval blockade of commercial shipping continues unchanged, and this month has already produced multiple "de-escalation" headlines (Thursday's mediation report, the earlier June ceasefire) that didn't hold. *Settling datapoint: the next Lloyd's List/PortWatch Hormuz transit count — the only hard, non-rhetorical measure of whether the pause is translating into vessels actually moving again.*

## 11. Journal Seed

The thing most worth forming your own view on this weekend is the CXMT IPO, because it forces a real question hiding inside the "Korea chip stocks fall" headline: is the AI-memory supercycle actually two different businesses wearing one ticker — HBM, where Samsung and SK Hynix still hold a 2-3 year moat, and commodity DRAM, where China is closing fast — or is the market right to treat both as one undifferentiated "China catches up" story? Before Wednesday's earnings calls, try writing down what fraction of Samsung's and SK Hynix's 2027 revenue you'd guess is genuinely HBM-protected versus commodity-exposed, then check your number against what management actually says.
