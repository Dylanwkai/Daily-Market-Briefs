## 2. Theme Tracker

### 2.1 fed-path — US Fed path & inflation — TURNING (hawkish reversal, and a fast one)
- **What changed:** CME FedWatch's implied probability of a 25bp September hike jumped to 74.5% (from 22.7% odds of a hold and 61.9% hike-implied readings as of Tuesday), after oil reversed sharply higher on the restrictive Hormuz draft terms (→2.2). This is the mirror image of Tuesday-Wednesday's move, when falling oil pulled hike odds down.
- **Market effect:** Dow fell 464 points (-0.85%), its worst session in over a week, snapping a five-day win streak; 10-year yield held roughly steady at ~4.63%; DXY stayed soft near 99.8-99.9 despite the repricing — a genuine disconnect worth watching (see concept line).
- **Concept:** Normally, higher rate-hike odds should strengthen the dollar — tighter policy makes dollar assets pay more relative to other currencies. That the DXY hasn't moved much yet, even after a 13-point overnight jump in hike odds, suggests currency markets are treating this as an oil-headline-driven repricing that could reverse just as fast, rather than a durable shift in the Fed's reaction function. Watch whether DXY catches up to the rates move over the next session or the rates move fades back toward it.

### 2.2 hormuz-oil — Hormuz / Iran war — TURNING (the actual draft terms are more restrictive than the market had priced)
- **What changed:** Iran's draft Hormuz shipping-route agreement with Oman surfaced with specifics: a bar on US and Israeli ships, and compensation demands from "hostile" nations before they could use the strait — a materially tougher ask than the market had assumed after Tuesday's coordinates-agreed headline. Brent jumped roughly 4% to $82.72 and WTI rose about 3.5% to $77.83, reversing a two-session, 10%+ decline. Trump had suggested resolution within "48 hours"; Washington's stated position (unrestricted navigation, no Iranian approval or tolls) is a long way from what Tehran is reportedly proposing.
- **Market effect:** Oil round-tripped from a two-day slide to a sharp single-session gain; equities gave back some of Tuesday's Hormuz-driven rally, concentrated in the more oil/rate-sensitive Dow rather than the earnings-driven Nasdaq.
- **Concept:** There's a difference between agreeing on logistics (shipping coordinates, which Iran and Oman settled Tuesday) and agreeing on the politics that make those logistics usable (who gets to sail, who pays, who has to sign off) — and markets had been pricing the former as if it settled the latter. When the actual draft terms surfaced and turned out to preserve Iranian leverage rather than remove it, the market correctly re-rated the odds of a near-term resolution lower, which is why oil round-tripped rather than just plateauing.

### 2.3 ai-capex — AI capex cycle — STRENGTHENING as a source of volatility (now spreading from logic to memory)
- **What changed:** Wednesday's AMD/SpaceX "beat but sold off" pattern (→prior briefing) repeated overnight in a different corner of the AI stack: SanDisk beat on revenue (+51% sequential to $8.97bn) and EPS, riding an AI-driven NAND pricing supercycle (gross margin 84.6% vs 26.4% a year ago), but guided Q1 FY2027 growth to decelerate sharply from that pace. Western Digital fell 13% on a weak first-quarter forecast; AppLovin tumbled almost 20% on mixed results the same night. The reaction crossed into Asia directly: Samsung fell roughly 6%, SK Hynix as much as 8%, and the Kospi dropped 4.6% — sharper than Wednesday's AMD/SpaceX-driven wobble, and this time hitting the underlying hardware names rather than just the US reporters.
- **Market effect:** Kospi -4.6% (worst session in weeks) with foreign investors turning net sellers; Nikkei -1.3% on the same chip-sector drag; Hang Seng -1.5%. Shanghai and the ASX 200, less exposed to the memory-chip complex, both ticked higher — evidence the move is sector-specific, not a regional risk-off shift (→1.4).
- **Concept:** Logic chips (GPUs, CPUs) and memory chips (NAND, DRAM/HBM) are different businesses with different economics — logic is about compute performance and customer relationships, memory is a more commoditized, cyclical business where pricing power comes from supply discipline across a handful of producers. Wednesday's AMD/SpaceX selloff was about capex discipline and customer concentration; today's memory-chip selloff is about whether an 84.6%-margin quarter is the new normal or a cyclical peak. Seeing scrutiny hit both sub-sectors within 48 hours is a stronger signal that the market's bar for "AI-exposed" earnings has risen across the whole stack, not just in one segment.

### 2.4 bond-regime — Bond market regime — TURNING (yesterday's "everything rallies together" pattern partly unwinds)
- **What changed:** VIX jumped to 20.66 from Tuesday's 15.5-16.65 range — its sharpest one-day move in weeks — while 10-year yields held roughly steady (~4.63%) and DXY stayed soft (~99.8-99.9) despite the hawkish repricing in Fed odds (→2.1). Gold held firm near $4,245-4,300; Bitcoin roughly flat near $64,000-64,500.
- **Market effect:** The unusual combination from earlier this week — falling yields, a soft dollar, firm gold, and firm equities all moving together — is starting to fracture. Equities and gold both look wobblier; the dollar hasn't yet responded to the hike-odds repricing at all.
- **Concept:** VIX is a real-time gauge of how much uncertainty options markets are pricing into the next 30 days, and it tends to jump fastest when a market that had been trading on one confident narrative (Hormuz de-escalation, AI-capex acceleration) suddenly has to process two pieces of contradicting evidence in short order — the restrictive draft terms and the memory-chip guide. A VIX jump without a matching move in yields or the dollar usually means the uncertainty is still being priced in equity-specific terms rather than as a macro regime change — worth watching whether rates and FX catch up over the next session.

## 5. Deep Dive — Memory Chips Crack: SanDisk, Western Digital and AppLovin Turn AI-Capex Skepticism From Logic Into Storage

**5.1 What happened:** SanDisk reported fiscal Q4 revenue of $8.97bn, up 51% sequentially and about 6.5% above consensus, with non-GAAP EPS of $39.25 beating estimates by roughly 13.5%. Data-center revenue, riding an AI-driven NAND pricing supercycle, surged 103% sequentially to $2.98bn, and non-GAAP gross margin rocketed to 84.6% from 26.4% a year earlier. Management also disclosed new long-term supply agreements worth a minimum $93.9bn with four-plus years of visibility, expected to cover more than half of bit shipments in FY2027. Despite all of that, shares fell after-hours because Q1 FY2027 guidance of $10.3bn-$10.8bn in revenue implied sequential growth decelerating sharply from Q4's 51% pace. The reaction spread fast: Western Digital, reporting the same evening, fell 13% on a weak first-quarter forecast, and AppLovin — unrelated to storage hardware, but reporting the same night — tumbled almost 20% on mixed results, reinforcing a "sell anything that guides cautiously" mood. By Thursday's Asia open, the contagion had crossed directly into memory hardware: SK Hynix fell as much as 8% and Samsung dropped roughly 6%, sending the Kospi down 4.6% on heavy foreign selling. ([TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/more/262081187-skhynix-samsung-semiconductor-stock-rout-tradingkey), [Zacks](https://zacks.com/stock/news/2969501/stock-market-news-for-aug-6-2026), [SanDisk investor relations](https://investor.sandisk.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sandisk-reports-fiscal-fourth-quarter-2026-financial-results))

**5.2 Why it matters:** This is the second consecutive session where genuinely strong AI-linked earnings triggered a sharp selloff (→2.3), but it's a meaningfully different sub-story than Wednesday's AMD/SpaceX moves. AMD and SpaceX were punished on customer-concentration and capex-discipline concerns tied to logic chips and compute infrastructure. SanDisk and Western Digital's stumble is about the memory and storage supply chain specifically — the NAND and HBM (high-bandwidth memory) pricing surge that's been inflating margins across the sector. When a company posts an 84.6% gross margin on a business that ran at 26.4% a year ago, the market starts asking how much of that is durable pricing power versus a cyclical spike that mean-reverts, and a guide implying deceleration — even from an extraordinary base — is exactly the kind of data point that answers that question the wrong way for a stock priced for continued acceleration.

**5.3 What's priced in:** SanDisk's roughly 500% year-to-date rally heading into this print already assumed the memory supercycle would keep compounding near its current pace; a guide showing sequential growth cooling from 51% to an implied 15-20% is a meaningful deceleration even though the absolute revenue level keeps climbing. Samsung and SK Hynix carry similar embedded assumptions in Korean markets, which is a large part of why the reaction there was, if anything, sharper than in SanDisk's own US trading.

**5.4 Monitor:** Whether Friday's session sees dip-buying in Samsung and SK Hynix — the way SoftBank and Arm got bought through Wednesday's AMD/SpaceX wobble (→2.3) — or the memory-specific skepticism persists into next week; whether any interim supplier commentary confirms the NAND/HBM pricing cycle is merely decelerating rather than turning over (Micron's next earnings aren't due until late September); and whether the "prove the ROI" scrutiny that's now hit both logic (AMD, SpaceX) and memory (SanDisk, Western Digital) within 48 hours starts compressing AI-capex-linked valuations more broadly, or stays contained to earnings-week idiosyncratic names.

## 6. Idea Lab — [[Western Digital]] (NASDAQ: WDC)

*Rotation to Western Digital — a pure-play hard-disk-drive maker, not a chip designer — after two straight days of Idea Lab picks in Japanese holding companies and US chipmakers, and the sharpest single-stock mover (-13%) in tonight's memory/storage earnings shock.*

**6.1 How it makes money:** Western Digital is now a pure-play hard disk drive (HDD) company, having spun off its flash/NAND business as the independent SanDisk in 2024. It sells across three segments: **Cloud** (high-capacity nearline HDDs sold to hyperscalers and data centers — the dominant and fastest-growing segment, riding AI-driven data-center storage demand for the "cold" bulk data GPUs don't need to touch constantly), **Client** (drives for PCs, gaming consoles and other devices), and **Consumer** (external and direct-to-consumer storage). Three KPIs matter most: (1) **exabytes shipped and revenue per exabyte** — HDDs compete on cost-per-terabyte via "areal density" (how much data fits per platter), so density upgrades drive both volume and pricing; (2) **Cloud segment mix**, since hyperscaler capex cycles now drive most incremental demand; (3) **gross margin trajectory**, since HDD pricing has strengthened this cycle as datacenter storage demand has outpaced supply — the same dynamic behind SanDisk's NAND margin expansion (→5).

> [!bullish]
> **6.2 Bull:** AI datacenters need massive, cheap bulk storage for training data and inference logs, and Western Digital is one of only three companies worldwide (with Seagate and Toshiba) making enterprise HDDs at scale — real pricing power in a supply-constrained market that isn't easy for a new entrant to contest.

> [!bearish]
> **6.3 Bear:** A 13% drop on guidance that merely didn't accelerate as fast as hoped shows the market has already priced in years of the AI-storage boom continuing uninterrupted, leaving little room for anything short of continuous upside surprises — exactly the dynamic that punished SanDisk, AMD and SpaceX this week (→2.3, 5).

**6.4 Differentiating question:** How much of Western Digital's recent margin expansion comes from structural pricing power in a supply-constrained HDD market (durable) versus a cyclical mix shift toward high-capacity cloud drives that could reverse if hyperscaler capex growth decelerates — and does this week's memory-chip guidance wobble (→2.3, 5) tell you anything about which one it is?

## 7. Trade Floor — Two Open Tensions

**7.1 FICC/Commodity — Oil round-tripped from a two-session slide to a sharp gain once Iran's actual draft Hormuz terms surfaced. Is the restrictive language (barring US/Israeli ships, demanding compensation) a negotiating opening bid that gets softened toward Washington's position, or the real shape of any eventual deal?** One side: draft terms are usually opening positions in a negotiation, not final terms, and Iran has already shown flexibility by dropping earlier demands for full bidirectional shipping control — this could simply be a starting point Oman talks down over the coming days. Other side: Iran retaining approval rights and compensation demands over "hostile" shipping is close to the core outcome Tehran has been signaling it wants throughout the crisis, and Washington's stated red line (zero Iranian approval or tolls) leaves very little room to converge without one side folding. *Settling datapoint: whether Tehran or Muscat walks back the US/Israeli-ship restriction in the next 48 hours, or Washington's response hardens into something more than another comment.*

**7.2 Equity — Memory and storage names (SanDisk, Western Digital, Samsung, SK Hynix) all sold off on guidance that beat or was in line but implied deceleration from extraordinary recent growth. Is this the AI-capex "prove the ROI" scrutiny (→2.3) spreading from logic chips into memory, or an idiosyncratic air-pocket in a NAND/HBM upcycle that's still structurally intact?** One side: seeing capex/growth-discipline scrutiny hit two different sub-sectors of the AI stack (logic on Wednesday, memory on Thursday) within 48 hours is a stronger signal than either move alone — a market-wide repricing of what "beat" needs to mean for AI-exposed names to hold their gains. Other side: SanDisk's own guide still implies revenue growing sequentially, just slower than an unsustainable 51% pace, and the underlying demand driver (AI datacenters needing more storage) hasn't changed at all — this reads more like profit-taking after a roughly 500% year-to-date run than a demand problem. *Settling datapoint: whether Samsung and SK Hynix recover on Friday the way SoftBank and Arm did after Wednesday's AMD/SpaceX dip, or the selling continues into a second session.*
